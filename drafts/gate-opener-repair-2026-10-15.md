## Why an Automatic Gate Stops Working

An automatic gate is a gate, a motor, a control board, a power supply, a set of safety sensors and a way to tell it what to do. A fault in any one of those can look the same from the driveway: the gate does nothing, or moves a little and stops. Sorting out which part failed is most of the repair.

**Power and controls.** A tripped breaker, a dead transformer or a failed control board will stop everything. Remote batteries and keypads fail more often than motors do, and they cost much less to fix. After a storm, a power surge can damage a control board, though a contractor has to confirm that on site. North Texas storms with 58 mph winds count as severe, according to the National Weather Service in Fort Worth, so a gate that quit after a storm is worth mentioning to the contractor.

**Safety sensors.** Photo eyes and reversing edges tell the gate to stop or reverse when something is in its path. A bumped post, dirt on a lens, a spider web or low sun can make a sensor think the path is blocked. A gate that opens but will not close is often reacting to a sensor, but it can also be a close timer setting or a damaged wire, and only testing tells you which.

**Mechanical wear and binding.** A motor can only push a gate that moves freely. A sagging swing gate, a bent arm, a seized roller or a track packed with soil and leaves makes the opener work harder until it trips, overheats or gives up. Replacing the motor does not fix a gate that is dragging on its own hinges.

**Ground movement.** Much of the Dallas area sits on expansive clay that swells and shrinks with moisture. A hinge post or a slide track slab can shift over a few seasons, and the gate falls out of alignment with its latch, its sensors or its track. That is a fence problem as much as an opener problem, which is why the cheapest honest fix is sometimes at the post, not the motor.

## Safe Checks Before You Call a Contractor

A few checks take ten minutes and give the contractor better information. They also keep you away from the parts that can hurt you.

1. **Remote and keypad.** Replace the remote battery. Try the keypad or wall button. If the wall button works and the remote does not, the problem is the remote.
2. **Power.** Check that the breaker has not tripped and that the opener shows signs of power, such as a status light. Reset a tripped breaker once. If it trips again, stop and call.
3. **Photo eyes.** Wipe each lens with a soft cloth and look for a status light on each unit. If one light is off or blinking, note it for the contractor.
4. **Obstructions.** Clear leaves, gravel, ice melt residue or a stuck toy from the track, the swing path and the bottom of the gate.
5. **Manual release.** Find the release and read the owner's manual before you use it. Only release a gate that is not moving, and keep people, pets and cars clear of its path.

Do not open the control box, jump wires or bypass a sensor to get the gate moving. The sensors are there to stop the gate on a person, a pet or a bumper, and a bypassed sensor can injure someone. Wiring that carries line voltage to an opener is electrical work. Texas Occupations Code [Chapter 1305](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/htm/OC.1305.htm) covers electrical licensing, so ask the installer who holds the electrical licence to do or sign off on the power side.

## Automatic Gate Repair: Fix the Opener, Replace It, or Rebuild the Gate

The question for the contractor is not "can you fix it" but "what failed, and what happens if I fix only that." A gate that is 15 or 20 years old may have a tired frame, worn hinges and an opener that is hard to get parts for. Wood gates sag and twist as the wood ages, and our page on [how long a wood fence lasts](/blog/how-long-does-a-wood-fence-last/) is a useful reality check before you spend money on a motor for a failing frame. If the gate itself is the weak link, the [gate repair](/gate-repair-dallas/) page and the [cost to replace a fence](/blog/cost-to-replace-fence/) guide show the other side of the decision.

Ask each contractor to price three things separately where they can: diagnosis, parts and labor. Ask what failed, whether the sensors will be tested, and whether they will show you the old part. Also ask who handles the power wiring and whether that person holds the electrical licence.

Texas does not license fence contractors at the state level. Some cities register contractors, including Dallas, Richardson and Irving, so ask for city registration where it applies, plus proof of insurance. Do not assume a firm is registered or insured because it says so on a website.

Check the rules before anything changes shape. A repair that swaps hardware is different from replacing the gate with a new style or height, and fence permits in Dallas depend on height and location. Confirm with the City and with your HOA before you change a gate. In many HOAs the association can still control fence type, so get written approval first. If the work involves digging for a new post or track slab, Texas law requires you or your contractor to contact [Texas811](https://texas811.org/homeowner/) two business days before digging, and if you hire a contractor, the call is the contractor's responsibility.

Then compare quotes line by line. Two or three comparable written quotes will show you which contractor found the cause and which just named a part. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does gate opener repair cost in Dallas?

It depends on the fault. One Austin-based firm advertises $150 to $400 for opener repair and $200 to $600 for motor repair, while a Dallas-area firm says early fixes run $100 to $300 and motor replacements $1,000 or more. Ranges differ between firms, so get two or three written quotes and compare what each one includes.

### Can I fix an automatic gate myself?

You can replace remote batteries, clean sensor lenses and clear the track. Leave the control box, motor and any line-voltage wiring to a professional. Power wiring is electrical work, so ask the installer who holds the electrical licence, and never bypass a safety sensor.

### Why does my gate open but not close?

The usual suspects are a blocked or misaligned photo eye, a close timer setting, or a damaged sensor wire. The opener is often fine. A contractor has to test the sensors and settings to know which one it is.

### Do I need a permit to repair a gate opener?

It depends on the work and the city. In Dallas, the City's fence permit checklist ties permits to fence height and location, and repairs are not described the same way in every city. Call the City and your HOA before you change the gate's size, style or position, and ask your contractor what applies to your address.
