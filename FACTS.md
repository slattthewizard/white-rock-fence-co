## FRAMING RULES (read before writing any copy)

1. A builder price is what ONE firm advertises, on a dated page. Write "one Dallas-area firm advertises $X". Never "our price",
   never "we charge", never a quote on this site's behalf.
2. National guides are labelled "national" (Fixr). Ergeon's multi-market pages are labelled "across Ergeon's installs", not Dallas.
3. Ranges on contractor blogs contradict each other, sometimes within one firm (see 1A: TX Fence & Deck says cedar $18-28 and
   also $15-30). Quote the range and the firm, never a midpoint. If two sources conflict, write around it ("prices vary widely").
4. If a number is missing, the site writes around it. Missing and NOT SOURCED: see the "COULD NOT SOURCE" block at the bottom.
5. Rules (permit, height) come only from sections 2-4. Always add "check with the city and your HOA before building".
6. The site never says it is licensed, bonded, insured, or that it installs, inspects, designs, or guarantees anything.
7. Never cross-link to the competitors in section 6.

Source reliability key: [PRIMARY] city/state/statute/government. [FIRM] a fence or gate company's own page. [GUIDE] third-party cost guide.
[AGG] aggregator, do not quote.

## 2. CITY OF DALLAS FENCE RULES [PRIMARY]

Dallas Development Code, Chapter 51A, Sec. 51A-4.602 "Fence, Screening and Visual Obstruction Regulations",
https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/dallas/latest/dallas_tx/0-0-0-83980 (fetched 15 Oct 2026). Last ordinance in the history line: 30893. Text:

- 51A-4.602(a)(2): "A person shall not erect or maintain a fence in a required yard more than nine feet above grade. In all residential districts except multifamily districts,
  a fence may not exceed four feet above grade when located in the required front yard, except when the required front yard is governed by the side or rear yard regulations pursuant to Section 51A-4.401."
  => Rear and side: 9 ft max. There is no separate 6 ft or 8 ft rear cap in the code; the permit trigger is 6 ft (below).
- (a)(3): "In single family districts, a fence panel with a surface area that is less than 50 percent open may not be located less than five feet from the front lot line."
- (a)(4)-(6): multifamily front yard fence up to 6 ft only if no lot in the blockface is single family or duplex, vehicle gates 20 ft from back of curb, and solid panels 5 ft back; otherwise 4 ft.
- (a)(7): height measured from the ground on the inside (rear/side yard); in a front yard, the greater of the inside and outside measurement; fill that alters grade counts.
- (a)(8): "A fence may not be located within an easement without the prior written approval by the agencies having interest in the easement."
- (a)(9) PROHIBITED materials: sheet metal; corrugated metal; fiberglass panels; plywood; plastic other than preformed pickets/panels with a minimum thickness of 7/8 inch;
  barbed wire and razor ribbon in residential districts other than A(A) Agricultural.
- (a)(10): "All fences must provide firefighting access to the side and rear yard."
- (a)(11): the Board of Adjustment may grant a special exception to the fence standards if it "will not adversely affect neighboring property".
- 51A-4.602(d) visual obstruction: no structure, berm, plant life "or any other item" between 2-1/2 ft and 8 ft in height measured from the top of the adjacent street curb, within a visibility triangle:
  45 ft along each curb line from the corner intersection (30 ft in central area districts, Deep Ellum PD 269, State-Thomas PD 225); and 20 ft at a driveway or alley edge and the street curb line (all districts).
  (d)(3): the Board "shall grant a special exception ... when ... the item will not constitute a traffic hazard."
  Dallas council briefing (24 Aug 2015) says the same in plain words: "Fences may not be placed in a visibility triangle ... if the fence is higher than 2-1/2 feet"
  (https://dallas.gov/government/Council%20Meeting%20Documents/qol_4_fences_combined_08242015.pdf).
- Penalty: Ordinance 30198 (passed 14 Sep 2016, the ordinance that wrote the prohibited-materials list): "punishable by a fine not to exceed $2,000"
  (https://dallascityhall.com/departments/sustainabledevelopment/planning/DCH%20Documents/Ordinance.Non%20req%20Fences_approved%209.14.16.pdf).

PERMIT [PRIMARY]: City of Dallas Planning & Development, "Fence Permit/Pool Barrier Permits ... Checklist", form 26-001 dated 6/10/2026,
https://www.dallascityhall.com/departments/sustainabledevelopment/DCH%20documents/FENCE_PERMIT_CHECKILST_26001.pdf :
- "A fence permit is required for fences exceeding 4 feet in height within a front yard or 6 feet in height in all other locations. The maximum allowable fence height is 9 feet."
- "Fences exceeding 9 feet in height require approval from the Dallas Board of Adjustment, a building permit, and plans sealed by a licensed professional engineer."
- Prohibited materials list (as above, "except where specifically permitted by code").
- Conservation and Historic Districts "will require the approval of a Certificate of Appropriateness"; "Designated floodplain areas may require additional approvals."
- Submittal: site plan to scale showing fence, gates, easements, fire lanes, driveways, sidewalks, alleys, curbs, visibility triangles; plat map; written utility authorization if in an easement; fence elevation drawing.
- Apply online through DallasNow (Residential Accessory Structure application). Phone (214) 948-4480; 320 E Jefferson Blvd.
- City guide "DallasNow: Applying for a Residential Fence or Retaining Wall Permit" (26 Aug 2025), https://dallascityhall.com/departments/sustainabledevelopment/DCH%20documents/7.%20Applying%20for%20a%20Residential%20Accessory%20Structure%20Permit.pdf :
  "You do not need a permit for fences or retaining walls that meet the following requirements except in areas, such as historic districts, conservation districts and planned development districts, which have additional requirements: Fences 4 ft or less in a front yard, or 6 ft or less elsewhere."
  Also: "Applications for fences and retaining walls require you to add a general contractor as the contractor." (Dallas wants a registered contractor on the application. Do not say whether a given firm is registered.)
POOL BARRIER: the same checklist says a separate pool barrier permit needs a site plan, barrier height/material/clearances and "the height and location of the gate latch release mechanism"; it does not state dimensions.
  Dallas pool barrier dimensions: NOT SOURCED. (Texas Health & Safety Code ch. 757 pool-yard rules apply only to multiunit rental complexes and property owners associations, not a single-family backyard pool; see 4D.)
PERMIT FEE: NOT SOURCED. The City's Permit Fee Schedule effective 1 Jul 2025 (https://dallas.gov/departments/sustainabledevelopment/buildinginspection/DCH%20documents/DSDFees%20(1).pdf) has no fence line item;
  its Table B-I (alterations/repairs) carries "Minimum permit fee is $175". Do not publish a Dallas fence permit fee.

## 3. NEIGHBOUR CITIES (permit / rear-yard height / front yard / source)

PLANO [PRIMARY, city handout "Residential Fence Permit - Requirements", Building Inspection Dept; undated PDF]
 https://content.civicplus.com/api/assets/c600ac9e-dfeb-4c68-adf1-0243dd3cb21e
 - Max height: "The maximum height of a fence shall not exceed 8'-0"." Front yard: "shall not exceed 40" in height and shall be at least 50% open."
 - Permit: a site plan is submitted with the application and a final inspection is required; "A permit is not required for repairs to existing fences not exceeding 25% of the area of the fence in a 12-month period."
 - Masonry fences, brick columns and retaining walls over 4'-0" need a Texas-registered engineer. Corner lots must keep sight visibility clips. Plywood, corrugated steel, barbed wire, fiberglass panels prohibited.
 - Fee: "Refer to the fee schedule" (NOT SOURCED).

GARLAND [PRIMARY, city page, read from the Internet Archive copy of 2 Jan 2026 because garlandtx.gov refused connections]
 https://web.archive.org/web/20260102134020/https://www.garlandtx.gov/2173/Fence
 - Permit required for: corner lot (any replace or install); swimming pool in rear yard; changing height, location or material; new fence where none existed. "Residential fence permit cost: $88 ($70 plus a $18 processing fee)".
 - "In a residential area, fences may be as tall as eight feet" (measured from grade inside the fence around the rear perimeter of the house).
 - Front yard: wood or wrought iron up to 3.5 ft with open spacing; brick/masonry up to 2.5 ft; "A permit is required for installing any front yard fence." Corner lots with a solid fence need a visibility clip.

## 4. TEXAS LICENSING AND LEGAL

4A. State licence for fence contractors. ANSWER: no state licence category was found. [PRIMARY, by absence] TDLR's "Regulated Industries" menu (https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ELECTRICIANS/exemptions.htm,
 navigation list, fetched 15 Oct 2026) names, among others, Electricians, Air Conditioning and Refrigeration Contractors, Water Well Drillers and Pump Installers, Elevator/Escalator Safety; it has no fence, gate or general-contractor category.
 [FIRM, secondary] Johnson Service Company (Fort Worth page, https://www.johnsonservicescompany.com/locations/dallas-ft-worth-tx/): "Texas has no state fence license and no state permit, so the rules for a fence in Fort Worth are the city's and the HOA's."
 No TDLR page saying "fence contractors are not licensed" was found. Site copy: "Texas does not license fence contractors at the state level; some cities register contractors (Dallas, Richardson, Irving). Ask a contractor for city registration and proof of insurance." Never say any firm "is licensed".
 City contractor registration: Dallas (DallasNow guide, section 2), Richardson, Irving (section 3). Plano, Garland, Mesquite, Carrollton: not stated in the fetched pages.

4B. Automatic gate opener wiring and TDLR electrician licensing. [PRIMARY statute; the gate-opener application is INFERENCE]
 - Texas Occupations Code Sec. 1305.151: "a person or business may not perform or offer to perform electrical work or residential appliance installation unless the person or business holds an appropriate license" (except under 1305.003).
 - Sec. 1305.002: "Electrical work" means any labor or material used in installing, maintaining, or extending an electrical wiring system ... "in, on, outside, or attached to a building, residence, structure, property, or premises".
 - Sec. 1305.003(a)(12) exempts "the design, installation, erection, repair, or alteration of Class 1, Class 2, or Class 3 remote control, signaling, or power-limited circuits ..." (TDLR exemptions page repeats this). (a)(6) exempts work in a dwelling by a person who owns and resides in it, unless a city ordinance regulates it.
 - Source: https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/OC/htm/OC.1305.htm and https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ELECTRICIANS/exemptions.htm .
 - INFERENCE: line-voltage power supplied to a gate operator is electrical work that needs a TDLR-licensed electrician; low-voltage control wiring (keypads, sensors) may fall under the (a)(12) exemption. Neither source mentions gate openers. The site must NOT state this as a rule; at most: "power wiring for a gate opener is electrical work; ask the installer who holds the electrical licence".

4C. HOA fence rules. [PRIMARY] Texas Property Code Sec. 202.023 "Security Measures" (added 2021 by H.B. 3571 and S.B. 1588; amended by S.B. 711, 89th Leg., eff. 1 Sep 2025),
 https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PR/htm/PR.202.htm :
 - (b) an association "may not adopt or enforce a restrictive covenant that prevents a property owner from building or installing security measures, including ... perimeter fence."
 - (c) it may still regulate "the type of fencing", bar fences that obstruct a license area, a public sidewalk or a drainage easement, require a driveway gate "set back at least 10 feet from the right-of-way" on a laned roadway, and (if the covenant says so) bar fencing in front of the front-most building line.
 - (d) fences built before 1 Sep 2025 may stay. (a) excludes condominiums and master mixed-use associations.
 Use: "In many HOAs the association can still control fence type, so get written approval first."

4D. Shared / boundary fences and pool fences.
 - No Texas statute on residential boundary-fence cost sharing was found. [secondary, legal aid] Texas Law Help, Disaster Manual Q. 3-7 (https://texaslawhelp.org/article/disaster-manual-section-3-falling-trees-flying-limbs-and-related-noises):
   "If both neighbors 'use' a fence straddling the boundary line ... both neighbors own that fence. As such, you and your neighbor would share the cost of repairing and replacing the fence." A fence wholly on one lot is that owner's. Site copy: general information, not legal advice.
 - Pool yard enclosure (48 in., 4 in. sphere, self-closing gate): Texas Health & Safety Code Sec. 757.002-.004 [PRIMARY, https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.757.htm] applies only to pools "owned, controlled, or maintained by the owner of a multiunit rental complex or by a property owners association". Do NOT cite it for a house pool; cite Richardson's 4 ft rule only as Richardson's.

4E. Sales tax on fence work. [PRIMARY, general only] Texas Comptroller Pub. 94-116 (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-116.php): "Labor to repair, remodel, or restore residential real property is not taxable";
 contractors may use lump-sum or separated contracts. Pub. 94-112 (https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/publications/94-112.php): landscaping "do[es] not include the construction or repair of decks, retaining walls, fences or pools ... These activities are either new construction, or repair or remodeling of real property."
 No residential fence-specific statement was found (the Comptroller's fence-labor answer is a farm/ranch FAQ). Site says nothing about sales tax.
